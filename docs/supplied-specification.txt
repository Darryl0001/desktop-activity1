Laboratory Activity 4 — Advanced Data-Driven Workflows and PostgreSQL Integration 
I. Activity Title and Overview 
Continue the Campus Equipment Borrowing System from Laboratories 1–3. Transition its operational database from SQLite to PostgreSQL, retain its working borrowing and return functions, and extend it with student/equipment management, coordinated multi-item checkout and useful history/search workflows. You are acting as a junior developer receiving the supplied requirements package: decide and justify the design, implement it, and demonstrate the expected behavior. 
II. Intended Learning Outcomes 
At the end of this activity, students should be able to: 
1. Interpret and implement a standardized actor-level use-case suite and business rules. 
2. Adapt an existing EF Core application to PostgreSQL and reproduce its relational schema with migrations. 3. Build persistent student and equipment management while preserving related history. 
4. Coordinate an atomic multi-item transaction and handle validation, stale data and persistence errors. 5. Retrieve and present related records asynchronously in responsive master-detail and search workflows. 6. Maintain MVVM and service/repository boundaries, verify integrated behavior and explain design decisions. 
III. Scenario / Problem Statement 
Laboratory staff must maintain student and equipment records, issue available equipment to eligible students and accept returns. They need to issue several items together without partial records, review student and equipment histories, and locate borrowing records efficiently. Preserve the Student, Equipment and Borrowing domain relationships from the earlier activities. 
IV. Functional Requirements 
Implement the five required continuations UC-01–05 and nine required new cases UC-06–09 and UC-12–16 in Appendix A and the separate Use Case Suite. Staff must be able to locate/select students and equipment within the relevant workflows. Student management includes registration, descriptive updates and eligibility; equipment management includes registration and descriptive updates. Borrowing history must include active and returned records. Search, filtering and sorting follow BR-17. Provide understandable success, rejection, empty, loading and error feedback. 
V. Use Case and Workflow Requirements 
The catalog, individually numbered business rules and fourteen tables in the separate Use Case Suite are the supplied specification appendix. Update your use-case diagram and catalog to reflect the scope and identify the custodian as primary actor. Submit a concise traceability matrix connecting the fourteen cases to your implemented behavior and verification evidence. You may adapt the supplied suite to document your solution, but must preserve its required fields, actor interactions, branches, rules and acceptance outcomes; do not create unnecessary include/extend relationships for

internal validation. No additional documentation for deferred cases is required beyond their catalog classification. 
VI. Technical and Architectural Requirements 
Use C#/.NET, Avalonia/XAML, CommunityToolkit.Mvvm, EF Core, PostgreSQL/Npgsql, and Git. Preserve the conceptual path Avalonia View → ViewModel → Application Service → Repository Abstraction → EF Core Infrastructure → PostgreSQL. Views and ViewModels must not run SQL, directly access the database, configure connections, or execute migrations. Design dependency injection and database-context lifetimes appropriate to asynchronous operations; explain your decisions. Use asynchronous database access where appropriate, especially history and borrowing-list loading, and keep the UI responsive. You may refactor models, services, interfaces, mappings, migrations, ViewModels and registrations with justification. Continue the existing project and preserve prior Git history where available. 
VII. PostgreSQL Transition Requirements 
Configure EF Core with Npgsql and use PostgreSQL as the operational database. Provide migrations that reproduce the schema on a clean database, appropriate keys, relationships and constraints, and adapted database-backed repositories. Persist and retrieve student, equipment and borrowing records. Provide sufficient safe initial data without overwriting an existing database on every startup. Document installation, local connection configuration and migrations; do not commit credentials. Simultaneous SQLite support and historical SQLite row migration are not required. Docker, cloud deployment and paid services are not required. 
VIII. Validation and Data Integrity 
Apply BR-01–19 and the use-case tables. The limit is three active items; the expected return date is later than checkout; registration defaults are described in BR-02 and BR-06. Revalidate current information at the transaction boundary. UC-06 commits all selected borrowings and availability changes or none. Enforce relationships, uniqueness and single-active-item integrity in PostgreSQL as well as providing useful application validation. Preserve history when editing or returning. Handle unknown commit outcomes as specified in BR-18. No hard delete, maintenance or retirement workflow is mandatory. 
IX. Expected Output 
A working desktop application backed by PostgreSQL with the fourteen required use cases, reproducible schema, clear feedback, persistent related records and defensible architectural decisions. A successful build alone is not evidence of working database operations.

Appendix B — Business Rules and Operational Policies The following rules govern the required workflows. 
Students and equipment 
- BR-01 — Student identity: Each student has a required institutional student number, unique after trimming leading/trailing whitespace and case-insensitive comparison. Empty or duplicate numbers are rejected by validation and database enforcement. 
- BR-02 — Student attributes: A student has a required nonblank full name and student number. Contact information is optional; if supplied, it must be nonblank after trimming. A newly registered student is eligible to borrow. No email format or additional personal data is required. 
- BR-03 — Student updates: Staff may edit the full name, optional contact information, and institutional number, subject to BR-01–02. A blank optional contact clears it. The stable internal identity and all existing borrowing relationships and history remain intact; eligibility changes use UC-14. 
- BR-04 — Eligibility changes: Staff may permit or restrict an existing student. Restriction prevents new borrowing, but does not cancel, erase, or prevent return of existing active borrowings. Repeating the current choice causes no unintended change and gives understandable feedback. 
- BR-05 — Equipment identity: Each physical item has a required, nonblank unique equipment identifier; trim surrounding whitespace and compare identifiers case-insensitively. Enforce uniqueness in validation and the database. 
- BR-06 — Equipment attributes and registration: An item has a required nonblank name and identifier, plus an optional description/type. A blank optional description is stored as absent. Newly registered equipment is available and has no active borrowing. 
- BR-07 — Equipment updates: Staff may edit identifier, name and description subject to BR-05–06; the stable internal identity and borrowing history remain intact. Availability, borrowing status and return dates are not editable descriptive fields; they change only through the applicable borrowing/return workflow. Maintenance and retirement are deferred. 
Borrowing and history 
- BR-08 — Eligibility and references: A borrowing requires an existing eligible student and existing selected equipment. A missing or stale reference is rejected without creating records. 
- BR-09 — Active limit: A student may have at most three active item borrowings. Existing active records plus distinct items requested must not exceed three. UC-03 counts one item; UC-06 counts all selected items. 
- BR-10 — Availability: An item is borrowable only if available and without an active borrowing. A successful issue makes it unavailable; a successful return makes it available. A database constraint or equivalent database-enforced protection must prevent more than one active borrowing for the same item. 
- BR-11 — Checkout date: The expected return date must be later than the checkout date, using consistently documented local calendar dates. An absent or invalid date is rejected. 
- BR-12 — Multi-item request: UC-06 requires at least two distinct items for one student and one expected return date. Reject duplicates and invalid selections rather than silently counting or omitting them. Create one active borrowing per item; a checkout parent entity is optional.
6 
- BR-13 — Return transition: Only an active borrowing may be returned. Record the actual return date as the local calendar date of return, which cannot precede checkout; a future expected date does not prevent early return. Mark the record returned, retain it, and make the item available. Reject a repeat return without changing state. 
- BR-14 — History and relationships: Each borrowing refers to exactly one existing student and one existing physical item through enforced foreign keys. History retains active and returned records, dates and relationships through edits and returns. Do not hard-delete students, equipment or borrowing history as part of required Lab 4 functionality. 
Integrity, retrieval and recovery 
- BR-15 — Revalidation and atomicity: Recheck current eligibility, active count, references and availability at the transaction boundary. A multi-item issue commits every borrowing and related availability change together or commits none. A conflicting update must not produce duplicate active borrowings or partial checkout; reject and refresh as appropriate. No distributed infrastructure or particular locking technique is prescribed. 
- BR-16 — Database integrity: PostgreSQL enforces primary keys, foreign keys, required fields, student/equipment identifier uniqueness and the single-active-borrowing protection. Consistent normalization of identifiers must match the uniqueness enforcement. Application validation supplements, not replaces, these constraints. 
- BR-17 — Search and results: Search by case-insensitive partial text in student name, institutional number or equipment name; trim surrounding search whitespace. Filter active, returned or all; sort by checkout date or expected return date, ascending or descending, with a stable borrowing identifier as a tie-breaker. Criteria may combine and can be cleared. Display matching records accurately; show an explicit empty state rather than an error for no matches. 
- BR-18 — Errors and unknown outcomes: Explain validation rejections and database failures without exposing credentials. Known failed writes leave no partial changes and do not report success. If connection loss makes commit outcome unknown, say so, do not automatically retry or assert rollback, and reconnect and refresh before a staff decision; the underlying transaction remains all-or-nothing. Let users retry after recovery. 
- BR-19 — Persistence and loading: Store and retrieve required student, equipment and borrowing changes in PostgreSQL across application restarts. Load database-backed histories and borrowing search asynchronously with observable loading/error state and a responsive window. Distinguish missing records, no matches and connection failures.
